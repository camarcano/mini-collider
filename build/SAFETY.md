# Safety notes

This is a children's build for a school event. Nothing here is dangerous *if*
you follow these notes. Print this page and keep it in the box.

## The three rules

1. **Batteries only, never mains.** Everything in this project runs on AA
   batteries at 6 V. Nothing gets plugged into the wall: not the board, not the
   buzzer, not the coil, not "just for a second". If a part only works from the
   mains, it is the wrong part.
2. **An adult owns the hot glue gun and the knife.** Cutting tubing, stripping
   wire, and hot glue are adult jobs. Children wind coils, tape, test, decorate,
   and label.
3. **Test before you glue, and supervise the stand.** A ball that shoots out of
   the cup can travel a metre or more.

## Specific hazards

**Small parts — choking.** Steel balls, coloured beads, and paperclips are all
choking hazards. Year 4 (age 8–9) is fine, but school events have **toddlers and
younger siblings**. Keep the spare balls in a sealed jar behind the stand, count
the balls in and count them out, and never leave loose beads on the floor. Have
a "sweep the floor before you leave" rule.

**Strong magnets.** Keep the finished coils and any loose magnets away from:
- **pacemakers and implanted medical devices** — a real risk; mention it at the stand,
- credit cards, hotel key cards, and phone wallets,
- phones, tablets, and laptop hard drives sitting on the same table.

**Pinch and projectile.** A steel ball hitting a hard cup is loud and can bounce
out. Keep faces and eyes behind the board line, never lean over the detector
while it is firing, and do not "see how hard we can make it go".

**Heat.** A coil left energised gets warm, and hot glue stays hot for a while.
Switch the gates off when nobody is using the board, and don't let children
handle a glue gun.

**Cuts.** The cut ends of plastic tubing can be sharp. Deburr the ends with
sandpaper or cover them with tape.

**Trips.** Tape the battery leads flat to the underside of the board. A stand
with a cable across the aisle is the classic school-fair accident.

**Batteries.** Never short a battery pack (a coil with a short in it can get hot
fast). If a wire gets warm, disconnect immediately and re-wind. Remove the
batteries for storage and transport.

## Before the fair

- [ ] Run it 10 times in a row with no failures
- [ ] Fresh batteries in everything, plus one spare set in the box
- [ ] Ball count written on the jar lid
- [ ] Cables taped down, board wedged so it cannot slide
- [ ] Loose beads only inside the detector cup
- [ ] A "do not touch the coils" sign, and the paperclip activity supervised
- [ ] Agree with the organisers where the board sits, and check the floor route to it

## If something goes wrong at the stand

Stop, unclip a battery lead (that kills all the power in one move), and tell a
teacher. There is nothing in this build that can hurt anyone while the batteries
are out.
