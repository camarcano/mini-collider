Colliderscope — board layout template

Print this page at 100% scale (A4 landscape, “no scaling”, no headers). Then copy the grid onto your board square by square.

How to use this

  1. Print at 100%. Every square on this plan is 10 cm × 10 cm on your real board, so the plan fits an 80 cm × 60 cm board.
  2. Rule a pencil grid of 10 cm squares onto the board (or onto lining paper stuck to it) and letter it the same way: columns A–H, rows 1–6.
  3. Copy the two grey lines square by square. A child can do this — it is like copying a picture from a grid, which they do in art lessons.
  4. The grey line is the middle of the tube. The tube is 16 mm across, so leave about 1 cm of space either side of your line.
  5. Drop a ball on the line and check it rolls the whole way before you fix any tube down.

The two pipes are mirror images of each other. That symmetry is not decoration: identical tracks are what makes the two balls arrive at the detector at the same moment, so they actually collide instead of arriving one after the other.

If a test ball stalls anywhere on the line, flatten that section out. Straighter and steeper beats curvy and clever — every time.

Labels to cut out and tape on

Print this page again, cut the boxes out, and tape them onto the board. A model with labels on it explains itself, which is the difference between a toy and a demonstration.
