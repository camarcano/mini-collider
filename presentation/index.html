Kid 1 — what is everything made of?

Everything is made of tiny dots

  • You, me, this table — all made of atoms.
  • Atoms are so small you cannot see them, even with a magnifying glass.
  • Inside an atom there is a nucleus.
  • Inside the nucleus there are protons.
  • And inside a proton? Even tinier dots called quarks.
Kid 1

How do we look inside something too small to see?

We smash it!

If you drop a water balloon on the floor, it splashes — and you find out what was inside.

Scientists smash tiny particles together at almost the speed of light and look at what flies out.

Kid 1

A collider is a racetrack for particles

  • Take a tiny particle — a proton.
  • Speed it up. Then speed it up again and again.
  • Use magnets to steer it round and round a giant ring.
  • Send a second beam the opposite way.
  • Then... CRASH! 🎉

Kid 2 — how does the machine work?

Magnets are the steering wheel

  • A proton going straight will never turn a corner.
  • Big electromagnets push it round the bend.
  • The faster the protons go, the stronger the magnets must be.
  • Try it in our simulation: turn the magnets down and the beam flies off the ring!
Kid 2

RF cavities are the push

  • Round and round is no good if the protons slow down.
  • Boxes called RF cavities use radio waves to give them a kick.
  • It is like surfing a wave — every lap, another push.
  • Our model: the plunger that fires the ball is our RF kick.

Kid 2

When they crash: E = mc²

Einstein worked out that energy can turn into matter.

So when two protons crash, all that energy makes brand new particles — particles that were not there before!

The detector is a giant camera that catches them.

Kid 3 — our model

This is what we built

  • Two clear tubes, wound into two spirals.
  • Steel balls = our protons.
  • They race round and round, getting faster, and meet in the middle.
  • CLACK! — the beads fly out. Those are our new particles.
  • A buzzer rings: detector signal!

Amazing but true

Three things that blew our minds

  • The beams pass through each other 11,000 times a second — but only about 20 in a billion protons actually crash.
  • The magnets have to be colder than space, otherwise they cannot carry enough electricity.
  • Some particles, called neutrinos, fly straight out of the detector without being seen at all.
What we learned

We learned that...

  • tiny things can be smashed to reveal even tinier things;
  • magnets can steer something you cannot see;
  • energy can turn into matter;
  • and scientists can build a machine 27 km long to answer one question: what is everything made of?
